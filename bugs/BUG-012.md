BUG-012

Scenario:
Live SITL flight (`waypoints` trajectory mode) using the climb-avoidance
strategy against the obstacle course, reported directly from observed
behavior: "the drone went straight, very slowly, into the GPS Denied
Zone and then went back in reverse."

Requirement violated:
No single existing REQ-ID names this failure mode directly, but it
undermines the intent behind REQ-CLEARANCE-01 and general mission
progress: an oscillating vehicle that never commits to an avoidance
maneuver never actually clears the obstacle it's reacting to.

Symptom:
Near an obstacle, the vehicle would creep forward, then reverse, then
creep forward again — an oscillation rather than a clean climb or
sidestep maneuver, instead of the confident, purposeful obstacle
crossings expected of a demo-quality avoidance system.

Expected behavior:
Once the climb-avoidance branch decides whether to climb over an
obstacle or sidestep around it, and picks a target height/direction, it
should hold that decision for the duration of the encounter — not
re-litigate it every control tick.

Actual behavior:
The climb branch recomputed the blocking-height/direction decision
(climb vs. left vs. right) from the vehicle's *current, moving*
position on every single tick. Because avoidance itself moves the
vehicle, each tick's new position could flip the decision made on the
previous tick, creating a self-reinforcing feedback loop: move → new
position looks different → decision flips → move back → repeat.

Root cause:
No "decision locking" existed for the climb branch. Unlike the
sensor-only bypass branch (which already computed its blind target once
and held it — see BUG-014's context), the climb/sidestep decision was
treated as stateless and recomputed fresh every tick from live position.

Fix:
Added `self._climb_target` state: the climb-vs-sidestep decision and
resulting target are computed once per obstacle encounter, stored, and
reused unconditionally on every subsequent tick until the obstacle
clears (`obs is None`) or a waypoint is reached — both of which reset
`self._climb_target = None` for the next encounter.

Regression test added:
`test_offboard_mission_node.py::test_climb_avoidance_target_is_locked_not_recomputed_each_tick`
— calls `_apply_avoidance` from two different positions that hit two
different real obstacles with different heights (one forcing
`can_climb=False`, one allowing `can_climb=True`) and asserts the
stored `_climb_target` is unchanged across both calls. First draft of
this test used the "left" obstacle-direction branch and passed even
with the bug present (a false-confidence gap caught before committing —
that branch's outcome doesn't depend on live sensor state without a
populated `_sensor_mins`, so it always fell through to the same
sidestep result regardless of the fix). Switched to the "front" branch,
whose outcome is deterministic and does depend on the fix, confirmed via
a debug script that the two chosen positions genuinely hit obstacles of
different heights (11.5m forcing sidestep, 6.0m allowing climb) before
finalizing the test.
