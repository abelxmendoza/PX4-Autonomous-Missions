Completion flags are not flight evidence.

A debugging case study from Abel Mendoza’s PX4 / ROS 2 Autonomy & Verification Platform.

Two cooperative recovery tests reported all tasks complete, yet the independent verifier rejected the recordings. The surviving drone passed about 0.8–0.9 metres from several task points and turned away. The coordinator accepted one metre; verification required an observed visit within 0.6 metres.

What exposed the bug

The unchanged kinematic tests exercised the real coordinator at 1.0 and 0.45 m/s. Completion and separation assertions passed. Position evidence failed. This was a deterministic coordinator acceptance defect, not missing telemetry or a timing race.

The fix keeps one-metre acceptance for transit corners but requires 0.5 metres at a task endpoint. Verification was not relaxed. New regression tests reject near misses and completion flags without observed travel.

Fresh simulation result

A new PX4/Gazebo recovery flight passed: 641 synchronized samples, two task reassignments, four point visits, both vehicles landed/disarmed, and 8.095491 metres minimum horizontal separation against a 2.5-metre requirement.

The 64-second replay uses this recording. A separate browser test also found that 10 Hz export could omit the exact terminal sample and display RETURNING instead of COMPLETE. Export now retains that final observation and recomputes the verdict from the raw input.

What this establishes — and its limits

Seven curated cases include five SITL recordings and two kinematic before/after traces. The preserved faulty trace must still fail. PASS means the documented checks passed; skipped requirements and absent triggering events do not establish behavior.

The flagship uses known-map routing, not LiDAR or camera navigation. Other single-vehicle modes include LiDAR processing and PX4 estimator integration using simulated external odometry under GPS-aiding loss. That odometry comes from Gazebo pose with modeled noise/drift, not visual SLAM or camera localization. ArUco detection is perception infrastructure and does not control the primary mission.

This is fixed two-vehicle, single-host SITL. It is not hardware/HIL validation, imagery coverage, payload delivery, or a continuous collision-safety proof.
